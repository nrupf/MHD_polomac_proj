\section{Plasma beta}
While pressure is the relevant parameter for fusion, analysis often uses $\beta$, 
the ratio of plasma pressure to magnetic pressure
\begin{equation}
    \beta \coloneqq \frac{p}{B^2/2\mu_0}
\end{equation}
For the Polomac prototype, using the design values from Elio et al. 2024 (Table 1),
plasma pressure ranges from $1.6$--$16$ kPa and magnetic pressure from $15.9$--$35.8$ kPa,
giving
\begin{equation*}
    \beta \in [0.04, 1]
\end{equation*}
A high $\beta$ is desirable for fusion efficiency, and the poloidal configuration is claimed to support 
higher $\beta$ than conventional toroidal devices (Elio et al. 2024). These are design 
targets and have not been experimentally verified.

\section{Alfvén waves}
Alfvén waves are transverse MHD waves propagating along magnetic field lines. 
To derive the dispersion relation, we linearize the ideal MHD equations around a 
static uniform equilibrium: $\vec{B} = B_0 \hat{e}_z$, $\varrho = \varrho_0$, 
$\vec{V} = 0$, $P = P_0$. We write each quantity as an equilibrium part plus a 
small perturbation, e.g. $\vec{B} = B_0\hat{e}_z + \vec{B}_1$.

To first order in the perturbations, the linearized momentum equation and 
induction equation (Ohm's law combined with Faraday's law) give
\begin{align}
    \varrho_0 \frac{\partial \vec{V}_1}{\partial t} &= \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\nabla \wedge \vec{B}_1) \wedge \vec{B}_0 \\
    \frac{\partial \vec{B}_1}{\partial t} &= \nabla \wedge (\vec{V}_1 \wedge \vec{B}_0)
\end{align}
Assuming plane wave solutions $\sim e^{i(\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x} - \omega t)}$ and 
considering perturbations perpendicular to $\vec{B}_0$ with $\vec{k} = k\hat{e}_z$, 
one obtains the dispersion relation
\begin{equation}
    \omega^2 = k_\parallel^2 v_A^2
\end{equation}
where $k_\parallel = \vec{k}\cdot\hat{b}$ is the wavenumber along the field and
\begin{equation}
    v_A = \frac{B}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varrho}}
\end{equation}
is the Alfvén velocity. For the Polomac, using $B = 0.25$ T and 
$\varrho = n m_i = 10^{20} \times 1.67\times10^{-27}$ kg m$^{-3}$ (Elio et al. 2024),
this was already computed in Section 2.5.1:
\begin{equation*}
    v_A = 5.5 \times 10^4 \text{ m s}^{-1}
\end{equation*}
The Alfvén velocity sets the characteristic speed of MHD disturbances and defines 

the MHD timescale $\tau_\text{MHD} = L/v_A$. It also enters directly into the 
Lundquist number below.

\section{Lundquist number}
The Lundquist number is defined as the ratio of the resistive diffusion timescale 
$\tau_R = \mu_0 L^2 / \eta$ to the Alfvén timescale $\tau_A = L/v_A$:
\begin{equation}
    S \coloneqq \frac{\tau_R}{\tau_A} = \frac{\mu_0 L v_A}{\eta}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, it can be written as the magnetic Reynolds number evaluated at the 
Alfvén speed. When $S \gg 1$, resistive diffusion is negligible on MHD timescales 
and the magnetic field is effectively frozen into the plasma, justifying the ideal 
MHD assumption of zero resistivity (Assumption 3, Section 2.5.1).

For the Polomac prototype, using $L \sim 0.3$ m, $v_A = 5.5\times10^4$ m s$^{-1}$, 
and $\eta \sim 10^{-6}$ $\Omega$m (Section 2.5.1):
\begin{equation*}
    S = \frac{4\pi\times10^{-7} \times 0.3 \times 5.5\times10^4}{10^{-6}} \approx 2\times10^4
\end{equation*}
This is at the lower end of the fusion-relevant range of $S \sim 10^2$--$10^8$ 
reported for laboratory plasmas \cite{wikipedia_lundquist}. While $S \gg 1$ confirms 
that ideal MHD is a reasonable starting point, the relatively modest value compared 
to large tokamaks ($S \sim 10^6$--$10^8$) reflects the small size and low temperature 
of the prototype. In particular, since $\eta \propto T^{-3/2}$, a reactor-scale 
Polomac operating at keV temperatures would have $S \sim 10^6$, well into the ideal 
MHD regime. For the present analysis, which concerns static equilibrium rather than 
dynamic phenomena, $S \sim 2\times10^4$ is sufficient to justify the ideal MHD 
framework (Freidberg 2014, Section 2.4).
