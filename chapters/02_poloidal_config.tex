\chapter{Specialization to poloidal configurations}

\section{Polomac geometry}
The magnetic field is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
    \vec{B} = \vec{B}_\mathrm{pol}(r,\varphi,z) + \vec{B}_\mathrm{tor}(r,\varphi,z),
\end{equation}
where $\vec{B}_\mathrm{pol} = B_r(r,\varphi,z) \hat{e}_r + B_z(r,\varphi,z) \hat{e}_z$
denotes the poloidal component and $\vec{B}_\mathrm{tor} = B_\varphi(r,\varphi,z) \hat{e}_\varphi$ 
denotes the toroidal component, directed along the azimuthal direction.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{figures/Polomac_Prototype_Elio2024}
    \caption{Cut out view of the Polomac prototype, figure taken from \cite{Elio2024}}
    \label{fig:polomac_cut_out}
\end{figure}

From \autoref{fig:polomac_cut_out} provided by \citeauthor{Elio2024}, we can see that the magnetic configuration 
of the Polomac is predominantly poloidal, so for $\vec{B} = B_r(r,\phi,z) \vec{e}_r + B_\phi(r,\phi,z) \vec{e}_\phi + B_z(r,\phi,z) \vec{e}_z$, 
we have that in the middle sections $B_z \gg B_r, B_\phi$ and in the top and bottom arcs $B_r \gg B_z, B_\phi$.
The total axisymmetry is broken in the middle section by the magnetic tunnels necessary to feed, support and cool the central solenoid. 
The device is described in greater detail in \cite{Elio2024}. The arcs are axisymmetric, i.e., invariant under rotation about the $z$-axis.


\section{Specialization to poloidal configurations}

Since $B_\varphi \ll |\vec{B}_\mathrm{pol}|$, all terms in the MHD equations 
arising from the toroidal field component enter as corrections of order 
$\varepsilon \equiv B_\varphi / |\vec{B}_\mathrm{pol}| \ll 1$. 
To leading order, the dynamics are governed by the poloidal field alone.
