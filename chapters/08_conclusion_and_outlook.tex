\subsection{Extension to non-axisymmetric perturbations}

The solver framework developed in \autoref{chap: Framework for a Minimal FDTD Solver for Linearized MHD of the Polomac}
assumes axisymmetry
($\partial/\partial\varphi = 0$), corresponding to the $m = 0$ toroidal mode.
Since the equilibrium itself is axisymmetric, perturbations of arbitrary toroidal
mode number $m$ can be incorporated without increasing the dimensionality of
the numerical problem. Writing every perturbation field as
\begin{align}
    Q_1(r, \varphi, z, t) = \hat{Q}_1(r, z, t)\, e^{im\varphi}, \qquad m \in \mathbb{Z},
\end{align}
replaces $\partial/\partial\varphi \to im$ everywhere in the linearized equations
\eqref{eq: lin continuity}--\eqref{eq: lin pressure}. Each mode number $m$ then
yields an independent 2D problem on the same $(r, z)$ grid, with additional $im/r$
terms appearing in the divergence and curl operators. The solver structure,
CFL condition, and boundary conditions carry over with only minor modifications.
The one qualitative change is that the azimuthal components $V_\varphi$ and
$B_\varphi^{(1)}$ no longer decouple from the poloidal components for $m \neq 0$,
so all six field components must be evolved simultaneously. At the axis $r = 0$,
the regularity condition becomes mode-dependent: fields must vanish there for
$m \geq 1$. Surveying the lowest few mode numbers would allow identification
of the dominant instability modes of the Polomac, which is of direct physical
interest given the closed-field-line geometry noted in \autoref{sec: conclusion}.

One way to start to consider tunnels would be setting the small perturbation of B phi dep.

8.2 Outlook
8.2.1 Numerical implementation

Implement the FDTD solver; verify with wave propagation tests (Alfvén, magnetosonic), ∇·B = 0 preservation, and energy conservation
Note the corrected 2D CFL: Δt ≤ Δx/(vf√2)

8.2.2 Non-axisymmetric perturbations

Extension to toroidal mode number m ≥ 1 by substitution ∂/∂φ → im; each m is an independent 2D problem on the same (r,z) grid
Physical motivation: the four magnetic tunnels break axisymmetry with four-fold symmetry (m = 4 and its harmonics); surveying low-m modes would identify dominant instabilities
This is the most direct route to assessing whether compressibility stabilization survives the tunnel-induced symmetry breaking — the central open physics question of the Polomac

8.2.3 Equilibrium beyond uniform background

The FDTD framework currently linearizes around a uniform B₀ê_z background; a natural next step is to use the Grad-Shafranov solution of Chapter 4 as the equilibrium and linearize around it
This requires variable-coefficient update equations and a non-uniform grid near the coil
